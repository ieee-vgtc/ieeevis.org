#### The Keynote
**Musical Juggling, Automata and Combinatorics**

Can a scientist help a juggler play music?
The musical juggling artist Vincent de Lavenère has spent a decade exploring the possibilities and challenges of a novel musical instrument composed of acoustic musical juggling balls that each play a note when caught. In order to train with his team, he needs some form of musical juggling scores. It is known since the 80s that juggling can be modeled by finite automata, but the real-world constraints pose various modelling and human-computer interaction challenges. The presentation is an adaptation from a wide-audience conference/show performed in schools, theaters, and scientific festivals. The goal is to illustrate, from an actual performing artist, the modeling methodology going through simplification, writing, computing, and simulation. 

Bio

Trained at the French Conservatoire National des Arts du Cirque, [Vincent de Lavenère](https://www.vincentdelavenere.com/) is a professional juggling artist with the company Chant-de-Balles. He is particularly interested in the interaction between juggling and music. He has created several musical performances, notably Ballade à 4 and Concerto for 4 Jugglers, in which the jugglers produce the music through the very act of juggling.

[Florent Hivert](https://perso.lisn.upsaclay.fr/hivert/) is a professor at University Paris-Saclay, specialist in algebraic combinatorics. His research focuses in part on finding concrete models for abstract algebraic computations, taking various forms, such as graphs, puzzles, and even 3D-printed stacks of shapes. He is also deeply involved in science outreach, particularly through his collaboration with Chant-de-Balles.
